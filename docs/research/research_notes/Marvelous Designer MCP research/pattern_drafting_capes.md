## 1. Cape and cloak types and how each is drafted (pieces, measurements, formulas, seams, sweep)

### Takeaway
Capes come in two drafting families. **Circle-fraction capes** (full, three-quarter, half, quarter) are annular sectors: the neck radius comes from the neckline length divided by 2π times the circle fraction, and the length is added radially. **Block-based capes** (shoulder-dart, shoulder-seam, panelled) are traced from bodice or coat blocks. Their front and back are pivoted apart at the shoulder point to add sweep, and their sweep can be far smaller than any circle (60 in / 1524 mm for a size-16 shoulder-dart cape). Cloaks are usually half circles with a hood joined at the neckline, matched centre back to the hood's centre seam.

**Circle-fraction capes: radius formulas**
- Full-circle radius = waist / (2π). Example: half waist 34 cm (340 mm) ÷ 3.14 = **108 mm**. Three-quarter = full radius + ⅓ of it = **144 mm**. Half circle = full radius × 2 = **216 mm**. Quarter circle = full radius × 4 = **432 mm**. Double circle = (W/2 ÷ 2) ÷ 3.14 = **54 mm**. — [The Shapes of Fabric, circle skirts](https://www.theshapesoffabric.com/2019/04/21/conquer-circle-skirt-patterns/)
- General rule: inner radius = waist / (2π × circle fraction). Equivalent forms are C/2π (full), 2C/3π (three-quarter), C/π (half) and 2C/π (quarter). — search-result synthesis of [Seam Whisperer calculator](https://seamwhisperer.com/circle-skirt-calculator/) and [calculate.co.nz](https://www.calculate.co.nz/circle-skirt-calculator.php) *(search snippet)*
- Cape-specific use of the same formula: the finished neck hole is the circumference. Example: 43 cm (430 mm) neck ÷ 6.28 = **68 mm** radius, drawn from the fold corner with a tape swung in an arc. The hem is marked the same way. — [Simply Sewing circle cape](https://simplysewingmag.com/sewing-patterns/how-to-sew-a-circle-cape-free-pattern) *(search snippet)*
- For a snug neck, divide the neck circumference by 6.28. With the pattern's 1 cm seam allowance the hole ends up "comfortably 1cm bigger all around". — [Cut Out + Keep, Christmas pudding cape](https://cutoutandkeep.net/projects/christmas-pudding-cape) *(search snippet)*
- A semicircle cloak's neck-hole radius = neck circumference (plus a bit) ÷ π. That is the half-circle rule. — summary of [Making Your Own Cloak, h2g2](https://www.h2g2.com/edited_entry/A42425480) *(search snippet)*
- Fabric use for a circle piece is radius + length. — search synthesis, same calculator sources *(search snippet)*

**Shoulder-dart (fitted) cape from a dress foundation, size 16**

### Inferences
- *Inferred formulas (from the circle rule above, with circle fraction f):* neck radius r = N / (2πf). Hem sweep S = 2πf(r + L). The flat piece is an annular sector of 360°·f between radii r and r + L. Worked example for neckline N = 480 mm and length L = 1100 mm (both illustrative):

  | type | f | neck radius r | hem sweep S | flat sector |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | full circle | 1 | 76.4 mm | 7392 mm | 360° (split at CF; usually 2–4 panels in real fabric) |
  | three-quarter | 0.75 | 101.9 mm | 5664 mm | 270° |
  | half circle | 0.5 | 152.8 mm | 3936 mm | 180° (straight edge = the two fronts) |
  | quarter circle | 0.25 | 305.6 mm | 2208 mm | 90° |
  | shoulder-dart cape (Pattern-Making.com, size 16) | — | from block | 1524 mm at 1067 mm length | traced from block |

- *Inferred:* Bucknell's 6 in neck radius on a half circle gives a neck edge of π × 152 mm = 479 mm. Its 3 in radius on a full circle gives the same 479 mm. Both agree with the r = N/(2πf) rule.
- *Inferred:* "Continuous over the shoulders" in the reference rules out a visible shoulder seam. That points to a circle-fraction body (full or three-quarter circle, split only at CB and/or CF) rather than a shoulder-dart cape. The pattern page does not say this.
- *Inferred:* the Studio Faro pivot (CB ⟂ CF, shoulder points touching) is the block-based way to get a half circle that still fits the shoulder. The shoulder slope is preserved and the side "seam" sits on the bisector through the shoulder point. Pivoting further apart moves towards three-quarter and full circles.

## 2. Arm slits and openings in capes (placement and finishing)

### Takeaway
There are three standard arm openings. The first is a **faced (window) slit**: facing strips are stitched about 7 mm either side of the slit line, the slit is cut to 15 mm from each end, clipped to the corners and turned. The second is a **slit left open in a vertical seam** (side-front or shoulder-line seam), with its edges bound or faced. The third is a **rectangular faced opening**. Slits are at least 200–250 mm long, near CF or on the shoulder line. In a lined cape the lining is slit in the same place and slip-stitched to the facing.

### Cited Findings
- Placement is personal, either nearer CF or on the "lengthened shoulder seam line". Mark one side on the body, then transfer it to the other side and to the lining. In this short cape the bottom of the opening is **100–150 mm above the hem**, and the opening is **at least 200–250 mm long**. — [Fairytale Cape sewalong, arm slits](https://thisblogisnotforyou.com/make-the-cape-adding-armslits-to-your-cape/)
- Faced slit: four strips (two per slit), **40–50 mm wide**, **slit length + 30 mm** (15 mm seam allowance each end). For example, a 200 mm slit needs strips of at least 230 mm. Place them right sides together on the marking. Stitch two lines **about 7 mm from the marking**, starting and ending at the marked ends. Cut along the line, **stopping 15 mm short of each end**, then cut diagonally to the stitching ends, leaving small triangles. Push the strips through and press. Stitch in the ditch, and stitch the triangles to the facing's seam allowance. — [same](https://thisblogisnotforyou.com/make-the-cape-adding-armslits-to-your-cape/)
- Lining the slit after the lining is in: pin the slit closed and pin the lining to the shell around it. Cut the lining open the same way, ending in triangles. Push the seam allowance between the layers and slip-stitch the lining to the facing by hand. Without a lining, the facing edges are catch-stitched to the cape. — [same](https://thisblogisnotforyou.com/make-the-cape-adding-armslits-to-your-cape/)
- Rectangular faced opening: put the armhole facing on the front panel right sides together, sew a rectangle through both layers, cut open, notch the corners, turn the facing to the wrong side and edge-stitch around. The lining is slit at the armhole and sewn into the armhole facing. — [Mood Winterberry](https://blog.moodfabrics.com/?p=36768)
- Slit in a seam: Threads' chic cape stops the side-front seam to form the arm opening. Edges are finished with bias tape, and the slit is topstitched along the tape's inside edge, starting at the hem, pivoting at the top of the opening and returning down the other side. — [Threads, how to sew a chic cape](https://www.threadsmagazine.com/2011/06/20/how-to-sew-a-chic-cape) and [PDF](https://www.threadsmagazine.com/membership/pdf/68649/chic-cape.pdf) *(search snippets)*
- Commercial example with "vertical slit openings in the front panels". — [Minerva model 5725](https://minervapatterns.com/model/5725?t=15) *(search snippet)*
- A double-welt bound slash (pocket technique) is another bound-opening method. — [Doina Alexei](https://www.doinaalexei.com/357-how-to-sew-a-double-bound-slash-pocket.html) *(search snippet)*

### Inferences
- *Inferred:* the cheapest slit to simulate is a **slit in a seam**. Draft the cape with a vertical seam where the slit should be (side-front, or a seam on the shoulder line running down the front), leave that seam unsewn over the slit length, and join shell to lining along the slit's two edges. A window slit needs an internal cut with corners, which is harder to build as a seam plan.
- *Inferred:* "100–150 mm above the hem" belongs to a short cape. On a long, below-the-knee cape a slit is placed where the hand falls, roughly waist to hip level, with its top around elbow height for arms hanging down. **In T-pose the arms leave the body horizontally at shoulder height.** A slit placed for arms-down will not line up with T-pose arms. Either the slit runs much higher on the shoulder line, or the avatar is posed arms-down before the slit is placed. This needs checking against the avatar in MD.

- No source gave a standard slit position in mm from CF or from the shoulder for a long cape. The sources say it is fitted on the body.

## 3. Collars: stand, "Dracula"/Medici/flared, Peter Pan, notched; drafting and interfacing

### Takeaway
A collar's **neck edge must equal the garment's neckline seam length**. Its **neck-edge curvature sets how it stands**. Ends curved up (shorter top edge) make the collar hug the neck, like a mandarin. A straight band stands vertical. Ends curved down (longer top edge) flare it outward; a semicircular fan is the classic vampire collar. A neck curve that copies the neckline lies flat (Peter Pan). Height comes from the style: 25–35 mm for a mandarin, about 200 mm (8 in) finished on even a child's vampire collar. The collar stands only with stiff interfacing (heavyweight fusible, hat interfacing), sometimes wire or board, and a full facing.

### Cited Findings
**General drafting principles**
- Measure the garment neckline on the stitching line, front and back, and make the collar's neck edge the same length. The neck-edge curvature sets the stand: "the straighter that edge, the higher the collar stands". A curve mirroring the neckline lies flatter. — [Minerva, collar drafting principles](https://minervapatterns.com/blog/creating-a-collar-pattern-types-and-drafting-principles) *(search snippet)*
- The garment neckline and the inside edge of the stand must be the same length. Measure CB to shoulder, then shoulder to CF. Stand heights quoted: 2–3 cm; women's 1 in (25 mm) stand with a 1.5–2 in (38–51 mm) fall; men's shirts 1–2 in (25–51 mm). Seam allowance is usually 1–1.5 cm all round. — search synthesis of [NIMI CITS trade theory p.164](https://nimi.co.in/cits_ebooks/Fashion_Design_Technology/Fashion_Design_Technology_TT/files/basic-html/page164.html), [Fabrics-store shirt collar](https://blog.fabrics-store.com/?p=10832) and [Doina Alexei stand collar](https://www.doinaalexei.com/beginnersewingtutorialblog/how-to-make-a-sewing-pattern-for-a-one-piece-rectangular-stand-collar) *(search snippets)*

**Mandarin / stand collar draft with numbers (The Creative Curator)**
- Baseline = neck edge. Square up at the left for CB. Mark **CB→shoulder 90 mm (3½ in)** and **shoulder→CF 105 mm (4⅛ in)**, both taken from the garment's own stitch line. Add the button stand: 15 mm if edges meet at CF, 30 mm if they overlap; the total from the shoulder notch is 120 mm in the example. **Raise the CF end 15 mm (⅝ in)** and curve from the shoulder point to that mark. **Height 35 mm (1⅜ in) at CB**, suggested 25–35 mm, kept parallel along the curve. Shape the end as a gentle curve, scallop or wing. Notches at **CB, shoulder and CF**. Seam allowance is **5 mm on the collar edge** and **10 mm on the neck edge**. Cut on the fold at CB, or reflect it into one long piece, in two layers. — [The Creative Curator, mandarin collar](https://www.thecreativecurator.com/draft-mandarin-collar-pattern/)

**Vampire / Dracula / Medici / flared high collars**
- For a "vampire-ish" collar, use "a semi-circular, fan-shape, cutting the interior curve the same length as the neckline", and test it in construction paper. A child's pattern has a finished collar "about 8 in [203 mm] high", from a 16 × 14 in (406 × 356 mm) piece. Another reply suggests a curve between straight and the neck curve, "with a minimal curve to maximize its ability to stand, and flared out corners", with florist wire or posterboard at the back, stiff hat-making interfacing and a full facing. — [Threads forum, adding a stand-up collar to a cape](https://www.threadsmagazine.com/forum/adding-a-stand-up-collar-to-a-cape) *(search snippets; page returned 403)*
- Very tall stands: the back of the head pushes the collar down at the back. One answer suggests grading the collar lower towards the back. — [Threads forum, very high standing collars](https://www.threadsmagazine.com/forum/very-high-standing-collars-pat-alts) *(search snippet)*
- The historical Medici collar stands upright at the back of the neck and opens at the front, supported by wire or heavy starch. — [FIT Fashion History, Medici collar](https://fashionhistory.fitnyc.edu/medici-collar/) *(search snippet)*
- A commercial Medici collar draft starts from a "flared and raised collar base". The result sits slightly raised, flares round the neck and rolls depending on fit. — [DP Studio, Medicis collar](https://dpstudio-fashion.com/en/collars/68-medicis-collar.html) *(search snippet; paid file)*
- Mood Winterberry's standing collar is about 16 in (406 mm) on the bottom edge and 18 in (457 mm) on the top edge, for a 15 in (381 mm) neck (per a comment), so the top edge is longer than the neck edge. It is cut double on the fold, folded lengthwise, side-stitched, clipped and turned. — [Mood Sewciety](https://blog.moodfabrics.com/?p=36768)
- SewHQ cape coat: the stand-up collar option is a **450 × 60 mm** strip. — [SewHQ cape coat](https://www.sewmag.co.uk/sewing-patterns/cape-cat-in-two-styles) *(search snippet)*

**Interfacing and stiffening**
- Heavyweight fusible interfacing in a vampire cape's stand collar. — [Favecrafts](https://favecrafts.com/Halloween-Crafts/Rigid-Collar-Vampire-Cape-Sewing-Tutorial)
- DRCOS: "iron a stiff fusible interlining to the collar lining fabric" before sewing the collar lining to the body lining. — [DRCOS vampire](https://dr-cos.info/fp-vampire.html)
- Black fusible interfacing fused to the collar before construction. — [FabricLink vampire cape](https://www.fabriclink.com/presentations/velcro/cape.cfm) *(search snippet)*
- SewHQ classic capelet: interfacing goes on the back of the collar. The collar is stitched round its outer edges at 1 cm, corners trimmed, turned, pressed, and attached to the neckline at 1 cm. — [SewHQ classic capelet](https://www.sewmag.co.uk/sewing-patterns/classic-capelet) *(search snippet)*

**Peter Pan and notched collars**
- A flat collar (Peter Pan) follows the neckline. A curve mirroring the neckline lies flatter. — [Minerva collar types](https://minervapatterns.com/blog/types-of-collars-from-peter-pan-to-mandarin) *(search snippet)*
- No usable free source on notched (lapel) collar drafting was retrieved in this pass.

### Inferences
- *Inferred geometry (cone model, not from a source):* treat a flared stand collar as an annular sector of inner radius R and angle φ. The neck edge is N = Rφ. Closed round a neck of circumference N, it forms a cone whose surface leans out from vertical by α, where **sin α = N / (2πR)**. Hence **R = N / (2π sin α)** and the **sector angle in degrees = 360° × sin α**. The **top edge = N + 2π sin α · h**, where h is the collar height.
  - α = 0° gives a straight rectangular band that stands vertical. α = 30° gives a **180° half-annulus**, which matches the forum's "semi-circular fan" vampire collar. α = 90° gives a full circle that lies flat, like a Peter Pan or circle capelet.
  - Example, N = 480 mm, h = 120 mm: at α = 20°, R = 223 mm, sector 123°, top edge 738 mm. At α = 30°, R = 153 mm, sector 180°, top edge 857 mm. The session's 38° flare would be a 222° sector.
  - Mood's 406 mm bottom / 457 mm top collar fits a nearly upright stand (about 5° for an assumed ~100 mm height), consistent with "standing".
- *Inferred:* a mandarin is the reverse case. Raising the CF ends (Creative Curator, 15 mm) puts the cone's apex above the neck, so the collar leans inward and hugs the neck.
- *Inferred:* "pointed tips" on the reference collar are the top front corners extended beyond the CF line. They can be modelled as extra points on the top edge near CF. No source gives dimensions.
- *Inferred, for MD:* interfacing has no direct analogue in MD. Stiffness comes from the fabric preset or extra layers. The session found that a two-layer leather and satin collar stood unaided (session report).

- No source with numbers for a Dracula/vampire collar draft for adults (height, flare, tip extension). Only the child's 8 in collar and the fan principle were found.
- Notched collar drafting was not covered.

## 4. Linings: bagged lining, attaching to collars and facings, how lining pieces differ

### Takeaway
Found cape patterns use **edge-to-edge bagging**. The shell and lining are each assembled separately (their own shoulder or panel seams), the collar is sandwiched or attached layer by layer, then the two layers are sewn right sides together round the whole perimeter, leaving about 127 mm (5 in) open at the back hem, and turned. In that construction **lining pieces are the shell pieces** (cut 2 fabric and cut 2 lining from the same piece). Jacket-style linings differ: a 25 mm CB ease pleat, the lining hem 25 mm shorter, a jump pleat. Capes use these only when the lining hangs free at the hem. The lining is never sewn to its shell along shoulder or panel seams.

### Cited Findings
**Edge-to-edge bagged capes (construction orders)**
- American Duchess 1910s wrap cape, numbered steps:
  1. Cut all pieces, **adding seam allowance**, and mark the darts.
  2. Stitch the three darts in both fabric fronts and both lining fronts, trim them to ½ in (13 mm) and press them down.
  3. **Stitch the shoulder seams for the exterior fabric, then repeat for the lining.**
  4. Stitch the cape sleeves to back and front, then repeat for the lining.
  5. Baste and press the seam allowances on the three straight edges of the collar contrast.
  6. Topstitch the contrast onto one collar piece.
  7. Place the two collar pieces right sides together and stitch round the three straight edges, **leaving the neckline open**.
  8. Turn and press.
  9. **Baste the collar round the cape neckline.**
  10. Place the entire fabric cape and lining right sides together and stitch round all edges **except about 5 in (127 mm) at the bottom edge of the back**. "This will also sandwich the collar between the two layers."
  11. Trim the corners and clip the neckline curve.
  12. Turn and press.
  13. Slip-stitch the gap.
  14. Add hooks and eyes.

  For velvet, which does not press well, topstitch the edges or hand-stitch the whole lining in. — [American Duchess PDF](https://downloads.americanduchess.com/1910s-wrap-cape-pattern.pdf)
- DRCOS vampire cape:
  1. Iron stiff fusible interlining to the collar lining, **sew the collar lining to the body lining** and press open.
  2. **Sew the shell collar to the shell body** and press open.
  3. Put shell and lining right sides together and sew round the perimeter, cut the corners, and **leave an opening at the hem**.
  4. Turn through the opening and iron.
  5. Topstitch round, closing the opening.

  — [DRCOS](https://dr-cos.info/fp-vampire.html)
- SewHQ classic capelet: make and attach the collar, then sew the lining to the cape round the neckline, front edges and curved hem at 1 cm, leaving a turning gap. — [SewHQ](https://www.sewmag.co.uk/sewing-patterns/classic-capelet) *(search snippet)*

**Part-bagged cape with free-hanging hem (Mood Winterberry)**
- Sequence:
  1. Shell: CB seam, then the front panels at the shoulder/side seams.
  2. Lining: front facing joined to the side-front lining pieces and topstitched. Lining front and back joined at the shoulder/side seams. CB seam.
  3. Collar sewn to the shell neckline, faces together.
  4. Collar finished (folded, sides stitched, turned).
  5. Lining pinned to the cape faces together and sewn along **CF edges and neckline**, "which also catches the unfinished collar edge".
  6. Armhole facings made.
  7. "Hem your main layer and lining layers separately."

  — [Mood Sewciety](https://blog.moodfabrics.com/?p=36768)

**Facings instead of lining**
- On a block-drafted cape the neckline and opening finish is "generally a facing", or the edges are bound. — [Studio Faro](https://www.studiofaro.com/drape-cape-top/)
- A hood lining's seam allowance is turned under and hand-stitched over the raw hood-to-cloak seam. — [Alley Cat Scratch](https://alleycatscratch.com/lotr/Things/FCloak/FCloak_pattern.htm)

**Jacket/coat-style lining drafting (where the lining is shorter than the shell)**
- Trace the shell pieces without seam allowance and draw in the facing lines. Raise the armholes ½ in (13 mm). **Remove 1 in (25 mm) from the lining hem**, then add a 5/8 in (16 mm) seam allowance. **Add 1 in (25 mm) at CB** (straight if the seam is shaped) and cut it on the fold as an ease pleat. Add seam allowances back to the shell pieces, with a 1½ in (38 mm) hem allowance on shell hems. Machine bagging: sew the lining like the shell, stitch it to the shell leaving a turning opening, turn, and hand-close. — [Seamwork, a lesson in lining](https://seamwork.com/articles/a-lesson-in-lining)
- A 1 in CB extension becomes a 2 in pleat once folded. — [Tilly and the Buttons](https://www.tillyandthebuttons.com/2010/10/sewaholic-tutorial-drafting-jacket.html?m=0) *(search snippet)*
- A 1 in basted pleat, pressed to one side and tacked at top and waist. — [sewing.org](https://www.sewing.org/html/lining2.html) *(search snippet)*
- A jump pleat is a small fold in the lining at the hem that opens when the wearer sits or bends. — [Threads, jump pleat](https://www.threadsmagazine.com/2025/05/13/add-a-jump-pleat-to-a-lining-at-the-hem) *(search snippet)*. No single standard depth was found.

### Inferences
- *Inferred seam ledger for a lined circle or shoulder-seam cape with a stand collar (DRCOS topology).* Every seam joins exactly two edges, which suits a simulator. S = shell, L = lining:
  1. Shell construction seams only: CB, and shoulder/panel seams if any. S↔S.
  2. Lining construction seams, the same list: L↔L. **These are separate seams; a lining is never sewn to its shell along them.**
  3. S_collar neck edge ↔ S body neckline, split at the shoulder notches into back neck and left/right front neck.
  4. L_collar neck edge ↔ L body neckline, split the same way.
  5. S_collar top edge ↔ L_collar top edge, and the two CF collar ends S↔L.
  6. S front edges ↔ L front edges, left and right, from the collar end to the hem.
  7. S hem ↔ L hem, all segments, for a fully bagged lining. Leave it unsewn for a free-hanging lining (Mood).
  8. Arm slits: S slit edges ↔ L slit edges.

  Edges that must stay **unsewn between shell and lining**: shoulder and panel seam lines, the body neckline (it meets the collar, not the other layer), and the interior of each piece.
- *Inferred:* the American Duchess topology (a finished collar sandwiched in the neckline seam) puts three layers on one seam. The DRCOS topology gives the same result with only two-edge seams. It also gives a collar whose face is shell fabric and whose inside is lining fabric, which matches "red lining throughout".
- *Inferred:* for an edge-to-edge bagged cape in simulation, lining pieces can share the shell outlines (as American Duchess cuts both from one piece). Jacket-style allowances (25 mm shorter hem, CB pleat) apply only to a free-hanging lining.
- *Inferred:* the session's failure is exactly the case this ledger forbids. The layer-clone created S↔L seams on every outline line, including shoulders and necklines, and no L↔L shoulder seams.

### Gaps
- No source gave a turning-gap length other than American Duchess's "5 in or so" (127 mm), which may need to be larger for bulky fabric.
- No cape-specific source on lining ease for velvet shells.

## 5. Seam allowances, notches, grainline and bias

### Takeaway
Home patterns use **15 mm (5/8 in)**. Industry and some indie patterns use **10 mm**. Enclosed seams (collars, facings) use **5–10 mm**, and hems **19–38 mm**. Some historical patterns include none. Notches mark CB, shoulder and CF and every matching point. Circle capes are cut with CF (or CB) on grain or on the fold, so the sides fall on the bias. They stretch, and real makers let them hang 24 hours or more before levelling the hem. Hood pieces must have a straight edge on grain.

### Cited Findings
- 5/8 in (15 mm) is the usual home-pattern allowance; Simplicity's standard is 5/8 in (1.5 cm). Industry often uses about 1 cm. Enclosed seams such as collars and facings use 1 cm, with 5–7 mm in one maker's facing practice. Curves use 6–10 mm to reduce bulk. — search synthesis of [Wikipedia, seam allowance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seam_allowance), [Minerva seam allowance](https://minervapatterns.com/blog/seam-allowance-explained-standards-choices-and-cad-settings), [Sew Direct](https://www.sewdirect.com/blog/tips-techniques/part-5-understanding-seam-allowance/) and [Ageberry](https://www.ageberry.com/seam-allowances-in-sewing-patterns/) *(search snippets; attribution to individual pages uncertain)*
- Per-pattern values found: Maridah 5/8 in ([Vampire set](https://maridah.shop/products/vampire-collar-cape-set)). Mood Winterberry ½ in, 12.7 mm ([Mood](https://blog.moodfabrics.com/?p=36768)). SewHQ capelet 1 cm ([SewHQ](https://www.sewmag.co.uk/sewing-patterns/classic-capelet), snippet). Creative Curator collar: 5 mm on the collar edge, 10 mm at the neck ([Creative Curator](https://www.thecreativecurator.com/draft-mandarin-collar-pattern/)). Seamwork: 5/8 in on seams, 1½ in (38 mm) on shell hems ([Seamwork](https://seamwork.com/articles/a-lesson-in-lining)). Fellowship cloak hem: ¾ in (19 mm) turned twice ([Alley Cat Scratch](https://alleycatscratch.com/lotr/Things/FCloak/FCloak_pattern.htm)). American Duchess: no seam allowance included ([PDF](https://downloads.americanduchess.com/1910s-wrap-cape-pattern.pdf)).
- A full-circle cape with one back seam: add 1.5 cm to W/2 for the seam allowance. One author adds the seam allowance before dividing by π "although apparently it's a no-no". — [The Shapes of Fabric](https://www.theshapesoffabric.com/2019/04/21/conquer-circle-skirt-patterns/)
- Grain and cutting. Half and three-quarter circles: **CF on the fold**, only a back seam. Full circle: cut twice, with two side seams. Short pieces have grain along CF/CB, and "Longer skirts must be cut in the other direction". The number of seams (2 or 4) depends on length and fabric width. Panels usually have the grainline in the middle, and mixed grain/bias edges stretch, so let it hang before adjusting. — [The Shapes of Fabric](https://www.theshapesoffabric.com/2019/04/21/conquer-circle-skirt-patterns/)
- Block-drafted cape: the grain kept on CF and CB lines. — [Studio Faro](https://www.studiofaro.com/drape-cape-top/)
- Hood kites: one straight edge on grain, or the hood "won't drape correctly". — [Alley Cat Scratch](https://alleycatscratch.com/lotr/Things/FCloak/FCloak_pattern.htm)
- Bias stretch. Hang the assembled circle at least 24 hours before marking the hem ([Omni Calculator](https://www.omnicalculator.com/tips-for-cutting-and-sewing-circle-skirt); [WeAllSew](https://weallsew.com/how-to-hem-a-circle-skirt/)). One costume guide recommends a week for bias-heavy pieces ([Baba Yaga costume hems](https://BabaYagaMusic.com/Dance-Costumes/Technique/making-costume-hems.htm)). Full circles stretch most, quarter circles least ([Omni](https://www.omnicalculator.com/tips-for-cutting-and-sewing-circle-skirt)). *(search snippets)*
- Notches. Collar notches at CB, shoulder and CF ([Creative Curator](https://www.thecreativecurator.com/draft-mandarin-collar-pattern/)). "Add seams and corresponding notches" on the cape ([Pattern-Making.com](https://pattern-making.com/drafts-hood-cape/)). Mark dead CB on the cloak to match the hood's centre seam, and pin from the centre out ([Alley Cat Scratch](https://alleycatscratch.com/lotr/Things/FCloak/FCloak_pattern.htm)). The collar end position is marked on the front wrap ([American Duchess](https://downloads.americanduchess.com/1910s-wrap-cape-pattern.pdf)).

### Inferences
- *Inferred:* in a simulator, a hem that comes out uneven after draping is the analogue of bias drop. Check it by measuring hem height round the body, not by trusting the drafted radius.
- *Inferred:* the notch set for a cape is CB, both shoulder points (where the neckline changes from back to front), CF, the collar-end mark, and the top and bottom of each arm slit. Each is a natural place to split an edge into seam segments.

## 6. Vocabulary of blocks, drafting systems, and open or machine-readable drafting sources

### Takeaway
The core blocks (slopers) are **bodice** (front and back, with shoulder and waist/bust darts), **sleeve**, **skirt** and **trousers**. Capes and coats are developed from bodice or coat blocks by pivoting darts. The major systems are Aldrich (UK, *Metric Pattern Cutting*), Armstrong (US, *Patternmaking for Fashion Design*), Bunka (Japan) and ESMOD (France); they differ in ease and silhouette. FreeSewing is the main open, parametric source. It has open blocks (Bella, Bent, Breanna, Brian, Noble, Sarah, Titan, Bibi) and one cloak (Lunetius lacerna) but no cape. No Seamly2D/Valentina cape file was found.

### Cited Findings
- FreeSewing blocks: **Bella** womenswear bodice block, **Bent** menswear body block with two-part sleeve, **Bibi** knit top body block, **Breanna** basic womenswear body block, **Brian** basic menswear body block, **Noble** princess-seam bodice block, **Sarah** skirt block "based on Aldrich", **Titan** unisex trouser block. Its only cloak is **Lunetius**, "a lacerna, a historical Roman cloak". Other relevant designs include **Sandy** circle skirt and three hoodies (Hannah, Huey, Hugo). There are no capes or ponchos. — [FreeSewing designs](https://freesewing.eu/designs) (freesewing.org now redirects to freesewing.eu)
- Lunetius' required measurements and cut layout: see question 1. The designer derived the curve "looking at stone reliefs". — [FreeSewing Lunetius](https://freesewing.eu/docs/designs/lunetius)
- Seamly2D/Valentina formats: measurements are `.vit` and patterns `.val`. Seamly opens Valentina `.val` files only up to version 0.6.1 (or 0.6.3). Passmarks (Valentina) and notches (Seamly) are implemented differently. — [Seamly forum](https://forum.seamly.io/t/get-patterns-from-valentina-to-seamly/6417); [Seamly wiki tutorial](https://wiki.seamly.io/wiki/Special:MobileDiff/2830) *(search snippets)*. No cape or cloak pattern file was found.
- A comparative study drafted bodices with **Bunka (Japan), ESMOD (France), Armstrong (US) and Aldrich (UK)**. Bunka: a slightly constricted waist. ESMOD: straight, least constricted. Armstrong: most constricted, rounded hips. Aldrich: a slight curve from bust to hips. — [IJCST, comparison of four basic pattern approaches](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/IJCST-03-2021-0038) *(search snippet)*
- A trouser comparison on a 3D-scanned subject: Armstrong fitted best and Bunka was preferred by the subject. Armstrong had the largest waist and hip allowances. — [Academia.edu trouser study](https://www.academia.edu/33323506/A_Comparative_Study_of_Trouser_Pattern_Making_Methods) *(search snippet)*
- Aldrich's bodice block has roughly 12.5 cm (5 in) ease at the underarm/upper bust. Aldrich uses UK sizing, and Helen Joseph-Armstrong uses US sizing. — [Dress Pattern Making, ease in Aldrich](https://dresspatternmaking.com/patternmaking-basics/analyzing-other-block-making-intro/ease-in-the-bodice-aldrich) *(search snippet)*
- Aldrich uses few measurements and is taught in UK and South African schools. Her armpit point sits halfway between the across-back and across-front lines, where other systems use ⅓ or 1 cm back. — [Seamly forum, bodice drafting](https://forum.seamly.io/t/yet-another-question-about-bodice-pattern-drafting/13062) *(search snippet)*
- Natalie Bray's *More Dress Pattern Designing* (p. 127) drafts capes from a coat block. — [Studio Faro](https://www.studiofaro.com/drape-cape-top/)
- Block vocabulary used in a cape/hood grading: scye depth, back width, neck point, shoulder, half scye width, chest width, front waist length, bust point, armhole, neckline, collar, hood. — [M. Müller & Sohn](https://muellerundsohn.com/en/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/308)
- Dart manipulation: pivot the block at the bust point until the original dart closes, then trace to the new location. The wedge stays the same and only its position changes. Darts can move to the shoulder, neckline, side seam, armhole or waist. — search synthesis of [Dress Pattern Making, bodice front style 12](https://dresspatternmaking.com/patternmaking-basics/principles-of-patternmaking/manipulating-darts/md-bodice-front/md-bf-style-12) *(search snippet)*. An armhole dart often shapes tops that fall into a flared hem. — [The Creative Curator, dart manipulation](https://www.thecreativecurator.com/dart-manipulation-types-of-darts/) *(search snippet)*
- Historical/costume cape and cloak sources with usable numbers: [Bucknell SCA semicircle cloak](https://eg.bucknell.edu/~lwittie/sca/garb/cloak.html), [Fellowship cloak](https://alleycatscratch.com/lotr/Things/FCloak/FCloak_pattern.htm), [American Duchess 1910s wrap cape (free PDF)](https://downloads.americanduchess.com/1910s-wrap-cape-pattern.pdf), [DRCOS vampire](https://dr-cos.info/fp-vampire.html), [Mood Winterberry (free)](https://blog.moodfabrics.com/?p=36768) and [Pattern-Making.com cape and hood](https://pattern-making.com/drafts-hood-cape/).

## 7. Translating to a 3D garment simulator (drafting for simulation)

### Takeaway
Simulators sew along the **sewing line**, so pieces are drafted **net (without seam allowance)**. Imported patterns that include allowance have the cutting and sewing lines swapped or the allowance stripped. Paired seam edges must be the **same length** unless easing is intended. Marvelous Designer flags a mismatch over **1 in (25.4 mm)** in red during free sewing. In segment sewing it silently **redistributes** a mismatch proportionally. Seam **direction** (directional notches) decides whether a piece is sewn flipped. Notches become segment split points.

### Cited Findings
- MD Free Sewing (2D): while you set the second half of a seam, the length difference shows in blue next to the cursor, and "If the difference exceeds 1 inch, the difference appears in red". The Directional Notch's facing depends on the order in which the start and end points are set. — [MD support, Free Sewing (2D)](https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358239854361-Free-Sewing-2D) *(search snippet)*
- MD M:N Segment Sewing: when the M segments' total length differs from the N segments' total, "N segments are divided in proportion to M segments". 1:N: the 1 segment "is equally divided by the number of N segments". — [MD M:N Segment Sewing](https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358238742553-M-N-Segment-Sewing-2D); [MD 1:N Segment Sewing](https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358225900825-1-N-Segment-Sewing-2D) *(search snippets)*
- MD Segment Sewing: when the Directional Notches are crossed, "either one of the Patterns is reversed and then sewn to the other Pattern". — [MD Segment Sewing (2D)](https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358199517209-Segment-Sewing-2D) *(search snippet)*
- The Epic Games MD sweater tutorial uses **Check Sewing Length**, then right-click → **Change Length** on a segment to match the armhole. — [Epic Games, sweater with MD](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/uefn/making-a-sweater-with-marvelous-designer-in-unreal-editor-for-fortnite) *(search snippet)*
- CLO (the same company's sister product): for DXF files that already include seam allowance, turn on "Swap cutting line and Sewing Line" and "Include Notches" on import, so the allowance disappears while you assemble in 3D. Add allowance back manually before re-exporting. — [CLO community, seam allowance as internal line](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/360037546313-Adding-Seam-Allowance-as-an-Internal-Line-Instead-of-External) *(search snippet)*. CLO's Seam Allowance tool is applied to an outline or internal line. — [CLO 2D Seam Allowance](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015714428) *(search snippet)*
- Style3D: setting seam allowance on DXF-imported patterns removes the existing allowance. — [Style3D seam allowance](https://help.style3d.com/studio/en/c2c8/5869/e875) *(search snippet)*
- A community opinion that MD "doesn't do seam allowances" unless built explicitly, and that turned seams can be faked with curved-side or double-sided geometry. — [Polycount discussion](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2270162/) *(search snippet; opinion, not documentation)*
- Real-garment practice that maps directly to simulation: match centre marks (CB of the cloak to the hood's seam) and ease a straight edge to a curve from the centre out ([Alley Cat Scratch](https://alleycatscratch.com/lotr/Things/FCloak/FCloak_pattern.htm)). Neck edge length equals neckline length ([Creative Curator](https://www.thecreativecurator.com/draft-mandarin-collar-pattern/)).
- Session evidence, local and measured: hem curves drifted about 2% short mid-hem through interpolated point moves. The layer clone created one seam group per outline line between clone and original. — `docs\sessions\2026-10-11-vampire-cape.md`

### Inferences
- *Inferred drafting rules for a model driving MD:*
  1. Draft **net shapes** in mm on the sewing line, with no seam allowance. Hem allowances and facings are not modelled as turned fabric.
  2. For every planned seam, compute both edge lengths from the drafted coordinates **before sewing**. Make them equal, or note the intended ease (for example, easing a straight hood edge to a curved neck).
  3. **Split edges at notches** (CB, shoulder, CF, slit ends) so each seam is a 1:1 segment pair, rather than relying on MD's proportional redistribution in M:N sewing.
  4. Fix **direction**: both edges run, say, CB→CF, or the piece is flipped.
  5. Keep a **ledger**: piece, edge, start/end, length, partner and purpose (question 4 gives a cape ledger).
  6. Read back the seams MD actually made and compare them with the ledger.
- *Inferred:* real fabric-width limits, such as panels for full circles or three panels for large sizes, do not bind in a simulator. Panel seams can still be kept, to match the real pattern and to make 3D arrangement easier, for example left and right halves arranged separately.
- *Inferred:* bias drop and "hang 24 h" have no direct simulator analogue. Fabric presets govern drape, so measure the hem after simulation.

- No 3D-garment-artist source found that discusses drafting capes specifically for simulation.
